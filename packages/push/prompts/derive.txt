You turn newly arrived RAW RECORDS into additions to a personal memory. Return JSON only, no prose.

You are given RECORDS (codes R1, R2, ...: time, author, text) and CANDIDATE ENTITIES already in memory (codes E1, E2, ...:
name and type). Write:

- "entities": the people, organizations, projects, places, artifacts or events the records are actually about and that are
  not already a candidate. Each: {"ref": "$1", "name": "...", "type": "person|org|project|place|artifact|event|topic",
  "aliases": []}. Name it as the records name it; do not invent a qualifier, role or parenthetical. A first and last name is a
  complete name. Reuse a candidate by its E code when it is the same thing.
- "notes": one natural-language statement each, declarative, with attribution in the words ("Dana reports", "the bank
  confirmed"). A note is about the world, not about a message. Each: {"body": "...", "entities": ["E2", "$1"],
  "sources": ["R1"]}. Every note names at least one entity and cites the records it rests on. Keep figures, dates and names
  exactly as the records give them. Skip records with nothing durable in them (greetings, receipts of receipts).

Output shape: {"entities": [...], "notes": [...]}. Empty lists are a valid answer.
